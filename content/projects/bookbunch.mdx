# Bookbunch

[Bookbunch](https://bookbunch.app) is a cosy home for small book clubs. I designed and built it on my own, from the database to the design, and it runs at **[bookbunch.app](https://bookbunch.app)** and as Android and iOS apps.

## Overview

Most book clubs live in a group chat, where spoilers slip out and nobody knows who's actually reading. Bookbunch gives each club, or bunch, a place of its own. Anyone can sign up, start a bunch or join one with an invite link, and inside they get:

- **A shared pace track:** everyone's bookmark on one line, showing who's ahead and who's behind the pace.
- **Margin notes:** notes tagged with a chapter and blurred until your own bookmark gets there, so you can share notes without spoiling anything.
- **Daily prompts:** a question about the book each day, with answers from the rest of the bunch.
- **Pick night:** whoever's turn it is brings three books in secret and runs the presentation live on everyone's screen, with ratings, a vote and a wheel to settle ties.
- **History and stats:** past books, ratings and who picked what.

Your personal reading shelf, yearly goal, reading streak and badges follow you across every bunch, and notifications let you know about new notes, the day's question and a streak about to run out.

## Tech Stack

- **App:** Expo and Expo Router (React Native) for Android, iOS and a static web export, with TanStack Query and theme tokens
- **API:** ASP.NET Core on .NET 9, Dapper, SQLite with migrations applied on boot
- **Auth:** Hand-rolled session auth with Argon2 hashing, bearer tokens on phones and cookies on the web
- **Notifications:** Expo push for the apps, Web Push (VAPID) for browsers
- **Data:** Open Library for covers and book metadata
- **DevOps:** Docker Compose, self-hosted on Coolify

## Role

I'm the sole developer and designer. I built the API, the data model and its scheduled jobs, the app across all three platforms, and the tooling around it: seed scripts for realistic demo clubs, characterisation tests that check the C# date and streak logic against the original TypeScript, and a Playwright-driven tool that photographs the running app to make its social posts. Accessibility was a launch requirement, so the app works with screen readers, keyboards and reduced motion.

## Why It Matters

Bookbunch shows the whole arc of a product: a single-club site that grew into a multi-club platform, moved from SvelteKit to one Expo codebase for web and mobile, and shipped with the polish of something people use every day. [Try it at bookbunch.app](https://bookbunch.app).
