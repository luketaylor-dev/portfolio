# VR Space Station Climber

A short **VR prototype** created to explore immersive climbing mechanics in a zero-gravity environment.  
The project used freely available **NASA space station assets** to create an authentic setting and focused on testing traversal systems inside VR.

## Overview

The prototype was built over one week as an experiment in movement mechanics and framework comparison. It served as a hands-on test of how different VR SDKs handle physics-driven climbing and player interaction.

## Key Features

- **Immersive VR Climbing** – designed mechanics for gripping, pulling, and traversing surfaces in zero gravity
- **Framework Comparison** – implemented climbing twice: once with the Meta/Unity XR SDK, and once with the **UltimateXR** package
- **Physics-Driven Interaction** – climbing relied on realistic force application and inertia, adding to the sense of immersion

## Technical Implementation

- Built in **Unity (C#)** with Meta SDK and Unity XR Interaction Toolkit
- Tested **UltimateXR** for faster prototyping and prebuilt climbing systems
- Integrated physics-based grabbing and momentum transfer
- Deployed for VR headsets to evaluate comfort, responsiveness, and usability

## Why It Matters

This project demonstrated how different VR frameworks approach the same gameplay challenge. By rapidly prototyping the same mechanic in multiple systems, it provided valuable insights into the trade-offs between **custom flexibility** and **preconfigured toolkits**.

## Future Potential

- Expansion into a full VR traversal demo with multiple environments
- Integration of multiplayer climbing or cooperative problem-solving
- Refinement of climbing comfort (fatigue, motion sickness mitigation)
